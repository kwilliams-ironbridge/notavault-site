## 1. The 10 most popular breathing techniques

Ranked by cultural reach: app-store presence, search interest, who popularized it.

| # | Technique | Pattern | Origin and how it got popular |
|---|-----------|---------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | Box breathing | 4 in, 4 hold, 4 out, 4 hold | US Navy SEAL training (Mark Divine), DHA's Tactical Breather app. The default "performance" breath. |
| 2 | 4-7-8 | 4 in, 7 hold, 8 out | Dr. Andrew Weil, 2015. Marketed as the sleep breath. Pranayama-derived. |
| 3 | Diaphragmatic (belly) breathing | Slow nasal breath into the belly | Physiotherapy, singing, and yoga. Taught in nearly every clinical setting. |
| 4 | Physiological sigh / cyclic sighing | Double inhale through nose, long exhale through mouth | Stanford (Huberman, Spiegel). Went viral 2021 to 2023 after the Cell Reports Medicine trial. |
| 5 | Wim Hof Method | 30 to 40 deep breaths, exhale, hold, repeat 3 rounds, often with cold | Wim Hof. Tummo-derived. Huge community, paid app. |
| 6 | Alternate nostril (Nadi Shodhana) | Close one nostril, inhale, switch, exhale | Hatha yoga. Popular in yoga studios and wellness media. |
| 7 | Coherent / resonance breathing | About 5 to 6 breaths per minute, even in and out | HRV biofeedback research (Lehrer, Vaschillo), Stephen Elliott's "Coherent Breathing". Core of HeartMath. |
| 8 | Pursed-lip breathing | Nasal inhale, slow exhale through pursed lips | Pulmonary rehab for COPD. The most prescribed clinical breath. |
| 9 | Buteyko | Reduced-volume nasal breathing, control pause holds | Konstantin Buteyko, 1950s. Revived by "Breath" (James Nestor, 2020) and Patrick McKeown. |
| 10 | Kapalabhati / breath of fire | Rapid forced exhales, passive inhales | Kundalini and Ashtanga yoga. Popular in classes and TikTok. |

## 2. The 10 techniques with the strongest scientific support

Ranked by quality of evidence and whether the outcome was measured objectively.

| # | Technique | Best evidence | Measured result | Benefit of building the skill |
|---|-----------|---------------|-----------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | Resonance / slow-paced breathing (about 6 bpm), the core of HRV biofeedback | Goessl et al. 2017 meta-analysis, 24 studies (HRV biofeedback for stress and anxiety). Steffen et al. 2017 RCT. Laborde et al. 2022 meta-analysis on slow-paced breathing and HRV. | Large effect on stress and anxiety (Hedges g about 0.8). Acute HRV increase, systolic blood pressure decrease, mood improvement in a single 15 minute session. | Raises vagal tone, lowers resting blood pressure, improves emotional regulation. Trainable and dose-responsive. |
| 2 | Cyclic sighing (physiological sigh) | Balban et al. 2023, Cell Reports Medicine. Randomized, 4 arms, 108 completers, 5 min/day for 28 days vs mindfulness. | Largest gain in positive affect of all four arms. Largest drop in resting respiratory rate. Effect grew with daily practice. | Fastest reliable state shift available (1 to 3 breaths to calm). Lowers resting breathing rate over weeks. |
| 3 | Diaphragmatic breathing | Ma et al. 2017, Frontiers in Psychology (RCT, 8 weeks). COPD meta-analysis of 15 RCTs, 1,098 participants. | Improved sustained attention, lower negative affect, lower salivary cortisol. In COPD: better FEV1, FVC, and 6 minute walk distance. | Foundational mechanics. Every other technique works better once the diaphragm is doing the work. |
| 4 | Pursed-lip breathing | Same COPD meta-analysis. European Respiratory Review 2024 systematic review. | Reduced breathlessness, better exercise tolerance, higher oxygen saturation during exertion. | Exhale control and CO2 tolerance. Clinically prescribed, near zero risk. |
| 5 | Breathwork as a category (slow, controlled protocols) | Fincham et al. 2023, Scientific Reports meta-analysis, 12 RCTs. | Small to medium reductions in stress (g about -0.35), anxiety (g about -0.32), depression (g about -0.40). | Confirms the category works when the practice is structured and repeated. |
| 6 | Wim Hof Method | Kox et al. 2014, PNAS. Almahayni and Hammond 2024, PLOS ONE systematic review of 8 trials. 2024 RCT, 84 women with high stress. | Higher epinephrine, higher IL-10, lower pro-inflammatory cytokines. Less post-stress rumination vs active control. Exercise performance results mixed. | Stress tolerance and inflammation response. Activating, not calming. Real fainting risk, never near water. |
| 7 | 4-7-8 breathing | Vierra et al. 2022, Physiological Reports (acute hemodynamics). 2025 scoping review in chronic disease. | Acute drop in heart rate and blood pressure in healthy adults. Sleep-onset evidence is mostly self-report. | Long-exhale skill for sleep onset. Modest evidence but strong user pull. |
| 8 | Box breathing | Included as an arm in Balban 2023. Widely used in military settings, few dedicated RCTs. | Improved mood and lower anxiety, but less than cyclic sighing in the same trial. | Attention anchoring under pressure. The performance-lane entry point. |
| 9 | Buteyko / reduced breathing | Cochrane-style reviews in asthma. Control pause (BOLT) used as the metric. | Fewer asthma symptoms and less rescue inhaler use. No change in lung function. | CO2 tolerance. The control pause is the best hardware-free progression metric there is. |
| 10 | Bhramari (humming) and alternate nostril | Weitzberg and Lundberg 2002 (humming raises nasal nitric oxide about 15 fold). Small HRV and BP trials for alternate nostril. | Acute HRV increase and small BP drops in small studies. A 2024 trial found no cognitive gain from humming. | Nasal health and a quick parasympathetic nudge. Evidence is thin and small-sample. |

Not on the list: Sudarshan Kriya Yoga has positive individual RCTs, but the pooled meta-analytic effect on depression is near zero (SMD 0.02). Kapalabhati has almost no outcome trials.

## 3. Cross-reference: which techniques are both popular AND proven

Scored 1 to 5. Weighted: evidence 35%, measurable result 25%, popularity 20%, app fit 20%.
App fit means a timer can guide it, a phone can measure it, and it has a progression ladder.

| Technique | Popular | Evidence | Measurable | App fit | Risk | Weighted | Verdict |
|-----------|---------|----------|------------|---------|------|----------|---------|
| Cyclic sighing | 5 | 5 | 5 (respiratory rate, mood) | 5 | none | 5.0 | Ship. Day-one win. |
| Resonance 6 bpm | 3 | 5 | 5 (HRV, BP) | 5 | none | 4.6 | Ship. The training backbone. |
| Box breathing | 5 | 3 | 3 | 5 | none | 3.9 | Ship. Performance-lane hook, ladder 4 to 6 count. |
| Diaphragmatic | 4 | 4 | 4 (cortisol, attention) | 3 | none | 3.8 | Ship as onboarding lesson, not a daily session. |
| 4-7-8 / extended exhale | 5 | 2 | 3 | 5 | low | 3.6 | Ship as the evening session. Frame as "long exhale," cite honestly. |
| Buteyko control pause | 2 | 3 | 5 (BOLT seconds) | 5 | low | 3.6 | Ship the TEST only. It is the progression meter. |
| Pursed-lip | 2 | 4 | 4 | 3 | none | 3.4 | Fold into the exhale drills. No separate module. |
| Wim Hof | 5 | 3 | 4 | 2 | high | 3.3 | Defer to v1.1. Needs safety gating. |
| Alternate nostril / humming | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | none | 2.2 | Defer. Nice to have. |
| Kapalabhati | 3 | 1 | 1 | 2 | medium | 1.6 | Skip. |

### The five that ship in the MVP

1. **Cyclic sighing** (5 min). The proof point: users feel it in one session, and it is the best-studied brief protocol.
2. **Resonance breathing** at 5 to 6 bpm (5 to 10 min). Where the long-term measurable gain lives.
3. **Box breathing** with a count ladder (4-4-4-4 up to 6-6-6-6). The technique people search for.
4. **Extended exhale** (4-8, graduating to 4-7-8) as the evening session.
5. **Diaphragmatic breathing** as a one-time guided lesson in onboarding.

### The three measurements that make "the graph"

No hardware. Each takes under 90 seconds, taken at baseline and re-tested weekly.

| Metric | How | Why it is defensible |
|--------|-----|----------------------|
| Resting respiratory rate | Count breaths for 60 seconds, tapping each inhale | Primary physiological outcome in the Stanford trial. Drops with practice. |
| Control pause (BOLT) | After a normal exhale, time until the first urge to breathe | Buteyko's standard CO2-tolerance measure. Rises with practice. |
| Longest comfortable exhale | Time a slow, comfortable exhale | Direct measure of exhale control, the skill under 3 of the 5 techniques. |

Self-rated calm before and after each session (1 to 10) is the fourth line, and it is the one users look at first.

### Claims we can make, and cannot

Allowed (each backed by a citation above): "lowers resting breathing rate," "improves mood in as little as 5 minutes a day," "raises heart-rate-variability when practiced at 6 breaths per minute," "reduces stress and anxiety scores in controlled trials."

Banned (carried over from Phase 3 section 6): no treatment or cure language, no medical HRV claims, no "clinically proven to treat anxiety," no d=1.44 outlier, no 29.6% CAGR. Wim Hof gets a safety screen before any content.
